A civic platform connecting Lebanese citizens with all 1,005 municipalities. Report a problem with photos, a map pin and voice dictation, follow the fix, sign petitions, vote and volunteer. Municipalities get AI-prioritised reports, and everyone gets a public budget and projects board.

The problem

Reporting a broken streetlight or a pothole to a municipality often means a phone call with no way to follow what happens next.

Baleegh gives citizens a way to report, track and rate a fix, and gives every municipality the tools to sort, answer and prove its work in public.

What I built

  • Reports with proof: up to four photos or the live camera, a map pin, and voice dictation in Arabic or English; every fix closes with a photo.
  • The full lifecycle, in public: Open → In progress → Resolved, with support, follows, comments, a 1–5 rating and a way to reopen a disputed fix.
  • Community tools per town: petitions that ask for an official response, polls, events, volunteer campaigns and a residents’ chat room.
  • Transparency boards: municipal projects with budgets and progress photos, and a water and power outage calendar.
  • Paperwork without the queue: online document requests with a received → in review → ready timeline.
  • Offices and admins: 1,005 pre-provisioned municipality accounts, citizen verification, duplicate merging, analytics, an SLA board and a full audit log.

How it works

Built on the TALL stack (Tailwind, Alpine.js, Laravel 12, Livewire 4), with no Node.js needed. One AI layer works with any OpenAI-compatible provider, Gemini’s free tier by default, and powers triage, photo checks, semantic duplicate detection, moderation, translation and a municipal assistant grounded in live data.

Every AI feature quietly switches off without a key, and every outside service (email, SMS, WhatsApp, push) degrades gracefully, so the app always runs.

Key decisions

  • Real data: all 8 governorates and 1,005 municipalities are seeded from the official UNHCR/MoIM dataset, in English and Arabic.
  • ID photos stay private: they are stored outside the web root and streamed only to authorised staff.
  • Scoped by design: a citizen can only act inside their own municipality, enforced on the server, and role and verification fields can’t be mass-assigned.
  • Staff sign-in needs two factors, and every staff action is written to an audit log.
  • Three languages: English, Arabic (right to left) and French, with 450+ strings each.
