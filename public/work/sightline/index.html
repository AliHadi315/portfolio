Sightline

The problem

For someone with low vision the questions are simple: what is on the desk, what does that sign say, has anything changed?

Sightline answers them out loud, in plain language, with directions like “to your left”, and without guessing anything it shouldn’t about people.

What I built

  • Scene mode: the most important thing first, then the layout and what is happening.
  • Read mode: every word in view, top to bottom, exactly as written: labels, signs, screens.
  • Only what changed: it remembers the last three descriptions and reports what is new instead of repeating itself.
  • Manual capture only: nothing is sent until you press a key or a button. No timers, no motion detection.
  • Two front ends, one core: a desktop app with a live camera window and a React website, sharing the same Python vision, speech and memory code.
  • Bring your own key: each visitor uses their own free Gemini key; without one, demo mode plays three samples.

How it works

One press sends a single frame, downscaled to 768 px, to the fastest free Gemini Flash model. If that model is rate-limited, overloaded or hangs, the call moves to fallback models straight away. The text appears the moment it arrives, and speech goes through a three-step chain, so there is always a voice.

one pressspace / r
  → one frame · 768 px · JPEG 80
  → Gemini Flash → fallback models on 429 · 503 · timeout
  → text on screen, then speech:
     edge-tts → Gemini TTS → browser voice

Key decisions

  • No guessing about people: never age, gender, race, mood or health; a person is described only by presence and action.
  • No frame archive: the website stores no photos and forgets your key when the request ends.
  • Built for free-tier limits: a 3-second cooldown, backoff on rate limits, an instant model switch on overload, and demo mode when the daily quota runs out.
  • A locked-down site: a strict Content-Security-Policy, the camera permission granted to the site only, and per-IP rate limits on the API.
  • Measured, not assumed: an eval harness checks the scene prompt against the objects expected in test frames.
